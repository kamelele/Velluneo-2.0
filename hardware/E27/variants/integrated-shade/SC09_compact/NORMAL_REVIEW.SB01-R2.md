# SB01 R2 — normal-operation calculation review

Actor: `/root/r2_normal_calcs`; 2026-11-24. This is calculation evidence for the existing first laboratory prototype, not approval, a measured result, or qualification of a finished lamp. Base commit: `872adddb3da9b3dbfa57e5c81ef7ba51da2a841c`. Exact source hashes are in `normal-calculations.json` and `handoff.json`. No canonical design file was edited.

## Decision

The selected components have plausible nominal normal-operation margins for a first bench prototype at 207–253 VAC, 50 Hz, with a 52–68 V LED string and 0–40°C room ambient. This normal calculation review identifies **no compulsory component or PCB change before ordering that prototype**. The exact purchasing and assembly review belongs to the coordinator. Normal operation has not been demonstrated physically; dynamic OVP, startup, hot behavior and transient immunity remain explicitly defined first-prototype tests, not claims silently passed by calculation.

The actual R2 input element in the legacy `L1` physical position is **PR02FS0201000KR500, 100 Ω, 2 W**, not the old 3.3 mH choke. T1 is **7687709152, WE-PD HV 1210, 1.5 mH**. Any active report still describing an L1 choke or applying its LC overshoot to R2 must be corrected before publishing a purchasing package. Keep the old calculation as history only.

## Output current and T1

The parallel 0.68 Ω / 3.65 Ω sense network is 0.573210 Ω. Applying the manufacturer's design equation gives 486.7 mA peak, 135.2 mA total average output, and approximately **133.4 mA into the external LED string at 60 V**, after the two 68 kΩ bleeders. Initial 25°C sense/threshold limits give 471.5–502.2 mA peak. An explicitly extended sensitivity adds 100 K resistor TCR excursion, 1% life reserve per resistor and 2% extra IC reserve: 447.2–529.7 mA peak, approximately **122–146 mA external LED current** over 52–68 V. These extra reserves are assumptions, not manufacturer guaranteed lifetime or hot-current limits; the factor 3.6 is a design approximation. A calibrated current surrogate independently predicts 132.4 mA at 230 VAC / 60 V.

T1's 0.52 A heating-current rating and 0.8 A typical 20°C saturation-current figure exceed the calculated normal currents. The periodic scenario reaches 0.199 A RMS; even a full-time triangular-current upper screen gives 0.306 A RMS. Maximum extended peak is 0.530 A. At 253 VAC / 52 V, a 0.972 mH low-inductance sensitivity (−20% tolerance, then two assumed −10% factors for hot/bias effects) requires 1.422 µs minimum ON time versus the IC's 1.25 µs maximum minimum-ON specification: 13.7% margin. The minimum effective inductance needed by that screen is 0.855 mH. Neither assumed extra inductance reduction nor the 20°C typical saturation curve establishes a guaranteed hot limit; inspect current ramp on the assembled board.

T1's normal running winding-voltage screen is 305.8 V; initial zero-output charging could approach 357.8 V before ringing. Both are below its 400 V working-voltage rating. The selected choke therefore remains appropriate for normal operation. Its maximum 20°C DCR extrapolated to 100°C gives at most 0.115 W copper loss in the periodic scenarios. Scaling its stated 40 K rise at 0.52 A predicts only about 5.9 K from this copper-current ratio; **that is not its assembled temperature**, because core loss, heat from adjacent parts and internal air temperature are additional.

## Actual RC input loss, voltage stress and capacitor ripple

`calculate_normal.py` replaces an unexplained fixed input-current assumption with 82 explicit line/current/tolerance scenarios. It uses a quasi-static rectified sine, a triangular buck current with a sin^1.5 peak envelope, the loaded resistive bus, Fourier harmonics and forward/open-bridge RC transfer functions. Capacitive line charging is added using a norm upper screen; the uncomputed Fourier tail is conservatively left unfiltered. Increasing 128 to 256 harmonics changes the controlling R10 loss from 0.52851 to 0.52587 W. The model is **not a manufacturer controller macromodel or a global nonlinear-rectifier bound**. It excludes burst/clamp control and parasitic ringing. Its auxiliary 0.3/1.2 W loss inputs are deliberately declared planning scenarios.

At 230 VAC / 60 V, R10 dissipates 0.247–0.290 W and RF1 0.131–0.152 W in these planning screens. Across the extended scenarios, maxima are **R10 0.529 W and RF1 0.257 W**, with RMS currents 68.5 and 71.5 mA. R10 has about 1.69 W continuous allowance even at a hypothetical 90°C local ambient under its linear derating curve. This is useful continuous-power margin; it neither predicts body temperature nor proves repeated hot surge endurance. Existing R2 startup pulse evidence remains separate.

Normal voltage screens, before ringing, are 357.8 V on C1/C4 and approximately 359.5 V on U1/D1. Their respective ratings are 1000 V, 630 V, 725 V and 1000 V. C4's 200 VAC rating is not compared incorrectly with the 253 VAC mains: the AC component of the unipolar rectified sine is about 110.1 V RMS. Its peak-current / minimum-capacitance slope screen is 3.92 V/µs versus 250 V/µs isolated-pulse capability. This supports retaining C4; neither that isolated-pulse specification nor a DC rating alone proves all-frequency dielectric heating. Check its temperature and bus waveform on the prototype.

The C6 periodic scenario predicts at most 0.137 A RMS ripple and 3.45 V peak-to-peak 100 Hz envelope ripple at its 120 µF tolerance minimum. This voltage-ripple calculation treats the output as a constant voltage with charge balance; it does not establish LED current ripple without a real LED dynamic resistance. Exact **EEUFS2A151** has 1.4 A ripple rating at 100 kHz / 105°C. The correct 47–330 µF row has factors 0.60 at 60 Hz, 0.70 at 120 Hz, 0.85 at 1 kHz, 0.95 at 10 kHz, 1.00 at 100 kHz. Using 0.60 conservatively gives **0.84 A**, ample above this scenario. Do not substitute the lower-rated, differently sized EEUFS2A151L row. Normal output up to 68 V plus a few volts ripple stays below 100 V; OVP is assessed separately below.

## Other normal dissipation

Maximum periodic screens: D1 forward loss 0.200 W; U1 MOS conduction at 100°C / 7.8 Ω, 0.089 W; R5 0.019 W; R6 0.0036 W; R7 0.029 W. Sense-resistor loss uses full inductor RMS as a conservative screen. R4 dissipates at most 0.00051 W at a 2.48 V threshold. Each bleeder dissipates 0.053 W at 60 V or at most 0.121 W at 90 V including initial resistance tolerance; selected 0.25 W parts remain suitable with prototype thermal checks. R7 normal peak voltage is approximately 306 V versus its 1600 V working rating.

Summed nominal component-loss screens total 1.09 W at 230 VAC / 60 V. An additional **assumed** 0.15–0.60 W for unmodelled core, switching and dielectric losses produces an engineering planning budget of **1.24–1.69 W driver heat**, approximately **82.5–86.5% efficiency** at the surrogate's 7.94 W external LED output. This is deliberately a budget, not a measured or guaranteed efficiency. The auxiliary loss used to boost the RC current is not added a second time. Component-screen totals across all corners range 0.90–1.51 W before the unmodelled-loss allowance. Neither these wattages nor a 0–40°C room specification establishes enclosed-board temperatures. Measure the actual local air, resistor bodies, C6 case, T1 and U1 temperatures.

## BP startup and output overvoltage

C5's 4.7 µF X7R / 25 V selection follows the controller's nominal recommendation and has ample voltage margin at the 5.55 V maximum BP shunt level. A conservative no-charge valley at 207 VAC / 68 V output lasts approximately 2.31 ms using the drain-regulator's 36 V headroom threshold. At the typical 180 µA standby current and an assumed effective 1.5 µF, droop is 0.277 V, below the 0.425 V minimum-BP-to-maximum-reset headroom. Required effective capacitance in that typical-current calculation is about 0.98 µF. A typical 2 mA charge estimate gives about 13 ms to 5.075 V with nominal 4.7 µF. No guaranteed minimum charging-current magnitude, maximum standby current or minimum biased capacitance was available in the inspected tables, so this is a **sensitivity check, not a guaranteed cold-start time**. Retain C5; measure BP minimum and startup at both line limits before enclosing the board.

The 390 kΩ / 12.4 kΩ OVP divider gives approximately 77.88 V nominal. Initial 25°C IC threshold and 1% resistor corners give 73.21–82.06 V. The retained extended engineering screen reaches 89.49 V. A single normal peak pulse stores at most 0.253 mJ using 1.8 mH and the extended current peak, raising a 120 µF output from that upper screen by only 0.024 V. However, continuing energy matters: a deliberately conservative 23.84 W normal-current envelope at 90 V consumes the available energy from 89.49 V to 100 V in about **5.01 ms**. The IC's maximum OVP response time is unspecified, so this establishes a measurable response budget, **not proof that dynamic OVP or fault auto-restart passes**. Scope output disconnect/OVP in the controlled first-prototype test. Do not infer protection against a 1.24 A fault event from the normal-current calculation.

Output-trip discrimination also needs the diode offset: Vout_trip = VOOV × (1 + R7/R4) − VF_OFF. At initial 25°C corners, using a 1.7 V diode screen lowers the minimum to **71.51 V**, above 68 V plus the model half-ripple (69.72 V peak). A consistent resistor-only TCR sensitivity with R7 ±200 ppm/K, R4 ±100 ppm/K, initial 1% tolerance, no extra aging reserve and the IC minimum still held at its 25°C 2.3 V gives minimum output trips of 70.45 V at |ΔT|50 K, 69.92 V at 75 K and **69.40 V at 100 K**. The last can overlap the 68 V output plus ripple. The hot IC OVP threshold has no specified bound in the inspected table, and the diode's 1.7 V screen is itself a 25°C/1 A catalogue value, not a guaranteed hot forward-drop ceiling. Therefore **normal regulation at the 68 V upper load end in hot conditions is conditional**. The retained legacy 69.37 V no-diode number cannot be declared a guaranteed hot output threshold. This review does not narrow the proposed 52–68 V window or relax the **90 V prototype stop/target**. Verify discrimination at the actual highest LED voltage and temperature; raising the divider now without that evidence would spend the limited OVP headroom. The 5.01 ms budget above goes to the capacitor absolute rating, not an acceptable relaxation of the 90 V test limit.

The natural unconstrained CrM surrogate requests up to about 53 µs ON time near line valleys in some 68 V cases; actual IC maximum-ON/minimum-frequency control will modify those parts of the waveform. This is a limitation of the current approximation, not an assertion that the controller violates its limits. It reinforces measuring real output current/ripple and BP at low line.
