# 8S output short and restart candidate

Task E27-8S-SHORT covers hard-short detection, startup qualification and temporary fault retention for REQ-CC and OPEN-CC. Codex owns this design study and its arithmetic. Base commit `af921647ac1db5d83a96fbfe6d81045046813917`. The [pin review](driver-pin-review.md) supplies the eFuse and flyback assumptions. This is a proposed protection circuit and test input. The schematic-stage requirements remain open.

## Detection and independent shutoff

- Use one undervoltage sense channel for the only LED output. Candidate TPS3700DDCR monitors the driver contacts through two series 150 kΩ resistors from LED+ and a 10 kΩ resistor to LED_RTN. Use 1% initial tolerances. One top resistor short still limits the sense voltage to below 2 V at a 30 V contact input. The divider receives no pull-up from the auxiliary supply, limiting backfeed into accessible contacts. Verify the comparator pin behavior when unpowered, PCB leakage and both divider faults.
- TPS3700 DDC pins are OUTA 1, GND 2, INA+ 3, INB− 4, VDD 5 and OUTB 6. INA+ is the LED divider, GND is LED_RTN and VDD is the held 3.3 V supply. OUTA is open drain and pulls low on undervoltage. Use INB−/OUTB as a candidate held-rail readiness monitor, with its polarity inverted in the control logic. Two series 255 kΩ resistors over 10 kΩ, all 1%, set a nominal rising threshold of 20.8 V. The initial rising screen is 20.19–21.43 V. This tests the hold capacitor directly. Treat readiness as a condition for entering the ramp. Its later deassertion must not clear an active or latched fault while the held 3.3 V supply remains valid. Budget resistor drift, leakage and the monitor reset hysteresis. Use pull-ups to the held 3.3 V rail and keep these sense nets separate from SHDN. [TI TPS3700, pin functions and electrical characteristics](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps3700.pdf#page=4) · [JLCPCB C33002](https://jlcpcb.com/partdetail/TexasInstruments-TPS3700DDCR/C33002)
- The initial calculated falling threshold is **11.76–12.65 V**, using TI's 387–400 mV range, ±25 nA sense current as a screening bound and resistor tolerance. The rising threshold is **12.03–12.78 V**. Include temperature tracking and PCB leakage before freezing the divider. This threshold is for hard shorts. A single shorted LED can leave voltage above this threshold and needs a separate current and thermal check. An open string still needs the flyback CV and eFuse OVP limits.
- S1 and TH1 directly inhibit SHDN when either opens. S2 remains the independent physical series cutoff. The short monitor adds a separate pull-down request at SHDN, with no ability to force it high when S1 or TH1 is open. Candidate start logic grants one supervised eFuse ramp after valid supply and S1/TH1 permit. Ignore the low contact voltage for an initial **25 ms** from ramp command. Once that window expires, latch off after **1 ms** continuously low. These are trial timing targets, subject to loaded turn-on, real comparator timing and pulse-energy tests. The eFuse protection remains active during blanking. The permitted initial low-voltage window is therefore about 26 ms in the nominal logic, plus analog and turn-off delays. Peak energy still depends on the actual current and voltage waveforms. The TPS26600 10 ms example at 47 nF is typical to 23.9 V. [TI TPS2660x, §7.6 and device functional modes](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps2660.pdf#page=9)
- In the latched state, S1 toggling, removal and reinsertion must leave the output off while the held rail is valid. Reset is proposed on loss of the retained supply followed by a valid cold start. This is a finite-retention reset design. If a RAW collapse under live mains can outlast retention, another ramp attempt is possible. Verify the distinction with actual power-cycle and sustained-fault traces before claiming mains-only reset.

## Retention and startup sizing

- Use a candidate diode and series charging resistor between RAW and a dedicated hold capacitor on the input of the 3.3 V regulator. The held branch is referenced to LED_RTN and stays upstream of the eFuse and S2. Isolate it from the permit drive and downstream load so it supplies only the monitor, latch, reset and SHDN pull-down control. Verify charge inrush and diode reverse leakage. Gate first enable on a charged hold capacitor, stable logic supply and a RAW voltage that permits the eFuse to ramp. This prevents first-starting a short before the hold capacitor has charged.
- Initial allocation is **33 µF minimum effective**, **150 µA maximum** across all loads including regulator current, pull-ups and leakage, and **20 V minimum at ramp enable**. Hold logic valid down to the provisional regulator input limit of **4 V**. The constant-current discharge bound is **3.52 s**. The LYTSwitch-6 ordinary auto-restart off-time is listed up to 2.11 s at 25°C. At the same capacitance and current, starting from only 14 V would yield 2.20 s, leaving little room for restart and recharge. Finalize timing over temperature with actual ramp and RAW recovery. [Power Integrations, auto-restart table](https://www.power.com/sites/default/files/documents/lytswitch-6_family_datasheet.pdf#page=26) · [TI TPS7A16, regulator input and dropout](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps7a16.pdf)
- Use a normally inactive permit drive and a local SHDN pull-down that sinks the eFuse internal pull-up. A pull-down transistor that only turns on after the logic powers up is insufficient by itself. The logic must power up with SHDN inhibited until its inputs and reset are valid. A valid latch must survive loss of RAW during the timing window. Evaluate a RAW collapse as an early failure of an already commanded ramp if the insertion permit stays true. S1/TH1 opening has immediate shutoff priority independently of latch state. The 3.3 V regulator pass-short, latch supply fault and SHDN sink open all need separate fault review. No logic parts or protection timings are approved at this point.

## Bench checks before approving the circuit

1. Verify minimum and maximum startup from 207 to 253 VAC, at the cold 8S high-voltage corner and at hot minimum forward voltage. Capture RAW, held rail, SHDN, FUSED and the contacts. Verify that the longest legitimate time below the falling threshold fits the shortest blanking interval.
2. Test a short present before enable, a short after steady state and a short during insertion with C6 precharged. Capture peak contact current, time to interruption, stress in U2 and R9/D8, and whether the hold rail stays valid. Repeat with the eFuse FET shorted as its own fault case while exercising independent S2 interruption.
3. Force RAW dropout during ramp and steady state, recover RAW before and after hold expires, then cycle S1 with the latch set. Record whether the output remains off with live mains. Set a minimum mains-off reset time based on measured retention spread. If a live-mains loss of RAW erases the latch, close the requirement with independent mains-presence detection or a reviewed bounded-retry design.
4. Repeat contact removal and thermal shutdown with S2 stuck closed, then check normal removal discharge separately. An eFuse OVP trip can still require RAW to fall below its rearm threshold. Confirm that mains reset also clears that state under no load. [OVP and recovery review](driver-pin-review.md#voltage-window-and-protection-behavior)
