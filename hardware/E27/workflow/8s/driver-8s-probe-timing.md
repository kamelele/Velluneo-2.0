## Probe path and two measurements

Candidate sequence: disconnect the main switch, discharge the contact node, then measure two separate pulses from a known initial state. Start with a weak pulse through 6.9 kΩ (the original 2 × 1.1 kΩ plus a 4.7 kΩ series resistor). Take a second measurement through 2.2 kΩ by bypassing only the extra 4.7 kΩ. Select the level before the hardware window opens and hold it throughout the sample. Loss of power must turn the probe path off and return the selector to weak. Both 1.1 kΩ current-limit resistors remain in series even if the selector fails short. The distinct switched discharge resistor is off during sampling. Measure actual RAW for each probe and verify that the output has discharged between pulses. A valid probe may request a supervised ramp. Independent contact protection must cover the case where a different load receives that same ramp.

The transient model assumes an ideal source, resistor corner tolerances, and a piecewise-linear 8S knee of 16–23.5 V with 40–300 Ω dynamic resistance. At 100 ms and 0.1–1.0 µF, the largest modeled difference between the strong and weak contact voltages is 0.881 V. Across the pure-resistor scan, the smallest difference for loads showing 16–24 V on the strong pulse is 3.753 V. These modeled ranges support further evaluation of a 2 V separation point. They do not establish a healthy production window. Short and open must also be excluded by absolute voltage, current and discharge checks. A low difference alone accepts both a settled open circuit and a hard short.

At 10 µF, a weak pulse into an open output reaches only 20.06 V after 100 ms at the lowest assumed input. The older discharge budget therefore cannot also serve as the sampling capacitance limit. Use 1 µF maximum as the initial test envelope for fast classification and check stray capacitance, external capacitance, effective local bypass, wiring and timing against it. At the assumed upper LED voltage and low RAW the weak current falls to 0.406 mA. The 0.22 Ω shunt produces only 89.3 µV, below the INA181 maximum 25°C input offset of 150 µV. Use the calibrated source and contact voltages and known probe resistance for a first current estimate during stable sampling. Budget ADC error, switch resistance, divider currents, tolerances and transient capacitance current. Keep the low-side shunt for full-power load detection. Obtain real forward-voltage curves at probe current before fixing thresholds. [TI INA181 specifications](https://www.ti.com/product/INA181)

## Hardware pulse and voltage limits

- Use a free-running LTC6991 oscillator as a bench reference with RSET 523 kΩ, DIVCODE 2, and positive polarity. Its 976 kΩ / 182 kΩ divider is from the manufacturer table. Use its rising clock edge alone to trigger an LTC6993-1 non-retriggerable one-shot with RSET 180 kΩ, DIVCODE 5, and positive polarity. Use the manufacturer's 1 MΩ / 523 kΩ divider. Each timer needs local 100 nF bypass. RST on the oscillator may be held at the fixed run level for the reference circuit. Route the pulse through the final enable gate, which also requires supply valid, output idle and probe request. The controller can veto a window but has no electrical route to generate clock edges. Gate the pulse after the one-shot, not the trigger signal. This topology still needs fail-off validation during rail ramp and rapid brownout, including a supervisor hold-off until both timers are valid. [ADI LTC6991](https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/data-sheets/ltc6991.pdf) · [ADI LTC6993](https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/data-sheets/ltc6993-6993-1-6993-2-6993-3-6993-4.pdf)
- Calculated pulse range is 114.10–121.88 ms with ±1% timing resistors and ±2.3% pulse accuracy. The normal minimum interval with the probe off is 542.16 ms with the conservative ±2.2% oscillator frequency corner. These are valid-supply, healthy-timer bounds. At a 30 V source and a hard short, the intact strong path draws at most 50.37 mJ from the source per pulse, averaging 75.85 mW at the shortest screened period. Delivered energy, contact heating and touch current require separate checks. A failed shorted switch or stalled timing resistor can violate the pulse bound. Check continuous dissipation at 30 V, including about 0.827 W in a surviving 1.1 kΩ resistor with the other resistor shorted as a stress sensitivity case. This sensitivity case combines loss of pulse control and a resistor fault. Preserve independent voltage and current limiting.
- Candidate probe cutoff is TPS3701 OUTB sensing RAW through 698 kΩ / 10.0 kΩ, both 0.1%. Use open-drain output to force the probe request low through hardware gating, with pull-up only to a valid logic supply. The calculated rising threshold is 28.03–28.61 V and minimum rearm is 27.33 V at the screening corners. The 0.26 V minimum rearm margin versus 27.07 V excludes ripple, TCR, errors of the real RAW range and switching dynamics. The 1.39 V maximum rising-threshold headroom versus 30 V still has to cover turn-off overshoot. This protects the probe branch only. Check detector power and the logic's default off path during an invalid supply, divider faults, high RAW, and a failed-short probe switch. Evaluate an upstream independent clamp or series cutoff for faults that bypass the local probe switch. The main-path overvoltage recovery issue remains separate. [TI TPS3701](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps3701.pdf)
