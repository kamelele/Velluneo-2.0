# 8S short-circuit restart decision and hardware-inhibit candidate

Scope E27-8S-RESTART, owned by Codex. Inputs are 8S-D3 and the brownout and 3D-placement study at `67f51cf93ae7dcd965bad49b616d5e7b5bc7730f`. This is a candidate-level follow-up. The [native D3 project](../../outputs/Zasilacz_8S_Draft/README.md) and the two physical interlocks remain the design references.

## Local inhibit during loss of the 3.3 V rail

Use one TPS3711DDCT supplied from RAW, ahead of the eFuse, for the next circuit experiment. Its open-drain output can be wired to SHDN with the existing R18 pull-down. This gives a separate sink while the source driving the enable loop is undefined. Leave the T1 isolation barrier intact. This circuit uses the existing secondary-side nodes.

- Pin 5 VDD to RAW with 100 nF local bypass to LED_RTN. Pins 2, 4 and 6 to LED_RTN. Pin 3 SENSE to the junction of 64.9 kΩ from V3V3_HOLD and 10.0 kΩ to LED_RTN. Pin 1 OUT to SHDN, downstream of R33. Use 1% initial resistors for this screening. No additional pull-up to RAW.
- The initial calculated falling threshold is **2.921–3.073 V**, rising **2.943–3.149 V**. The maximum rising threshold leaves **85 mV** against the 3.234 V LDO low limit before rail ripple, temperature drift, offsets and board leakage. Both detector thresholds lie above the 1.65 V lower operating limit of U107. Verify sequencing hysteresis and both absolute thresholds on a slow ramp.
- Using R33 at its −1% corner and 3.366 V at the input, the conservative sink demand at 0.25 V is 3.15 mA plus eFuse pin current. This is above the TPS3711 3 mA low-level test point at 1.8 V and below its 5 mA test point at 5 V. For guaranteed screening use 1.2 kΩ for R33 (−1%) to bring the demand to about 2.62 mA including a 10 µA eFuse allowance. Verify the resulting SHDN-high margin and actual pull-down with S1/TH1 closed. Check RAW surges against the detector's 36 V operating and 40 V absolute maximum supply limits, ground offset to the eFuse, and interaction between R33, the detector clamp and the timer. A shorted R33 can remove the current limit between the permit driver and OUT. Test that fault and a harness injection separately. While RAW is below the operating range of the detector, the eFuse input undervoltage lockout and the existing passive pull-down must keep the output off. Verify that overlap during ramp and recovery.
- This inhibit addresses slow or fast collapse of the logic rail while RAW remains in range. It does not retain fault state across loss of the held rail. Qualification needs a concurrent RAW/3.3 V ramp, a U101 pass-short fault and a persistent output short. ## Restart policy

**Use retained lockout as the proposed design basis pending the product reset decision.** D3's volatile latch permits another startup after enough time below POR. A shared error or dropout of RAW and 3.3 V defeats a reset policy based only on the held supply. A true mains-cycle reset needs a durable indication of a qualified mains-off event. An optocoupler reporting mains presence is one ingredient. The receiving circuit also needs power while it recognizes and records mains loss. Keep this as a separate feasibility task because the isolation, additional power, fail state and space impact require validation. [Optocoupler candidate family](https://www.vishay.com/docs/82424/vol617a.pdf)

A service-reset alternative can use nonvolatile state to disarm even with all rails lost. A write issued only after detecting a short has a power-loss race. For evaluation, write and verify a disarmed marker before the first enable and keep it through the on-cycle. Permit only in live state with the marker verified. Then an unexpected reboot enters lockout by default. This variant also locks out after an ordinary power loss unless separate power-fail sequencing records a validated shutdown. Validate incomplete writes, endurance, watchdog/reset behavior and programming, and retain independent hard S1/TH1 and S2 cutoff. ATtiny402 is a sourcing candidate for this alternative, and no firmware or reset method is adopted here. [Microchip ATtiny402](https://www.microchip.com/en-us/product/ATTINY402) · [JLCPCB C1339884](https://jlcpcb.com/partdetail/MicrochipTech-ATTINY402SSN/C1339884)

A bounded retry must specify the maximum attempt count, minimum interval and allowable current/energy at each stressed component and at the contacts. Store the attempt count across control rail loss for a finite retry budget. A TPS26601 latch alone cannot replace this function because the flyback may limit current below the eFuse trip and a SHDN cycle can clear the latch. Use 0.207 J of stored RAW capacitor energy as an inventory screen for 470 µF +20% at 27.07 V. A separate 27.07 V × 285 mA × 37.6 ms screen gives 0.290 J from a hypothetical source at those conditions. These are not output pulse measurements or guaranteed maxima. Capture actual peak current, duration and integral of voltage times current across the eFuse, wiring and short, including the highest voltage, hot replug and repeatedly collapsing RAW. Without those data there is no validated retry limit. [TI TPS2660x operating modes](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps2660.pdf) · [D3 timing spread](driver-8s-d3-review.md)

## Checked evidence

Run `python work/check_8s_restart.py` from hardware/E27. The calculation checks divider corners, capacitor inventory and the static state-policy distinction. It demonstrates that a recovered supply with closed interlocks can run after a volatile reset while a retained fault still holds off. It does not simulate an LDO, eFuse, memory, or transistor. The next capture should add the hardware inhibit and prove its dynamics before setting an approved restart behavior. PCB routing remains pending.
