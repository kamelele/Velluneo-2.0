# Two-contact LED output and automatic fault recovery

Task E27-8S-TWO-CONTACT. Owner decision after 8S-D4 is to remove the insertion interlocks and user reset, tolerate live unplugging and reconnection of the 8S load, and resume automatically after a cleared short. Codex owns this requirements and architecture update. Base is Velluneo commit 00e32cf518c72beb2ce4053b342cb536ba399207 and the [existing D4 KiCad baseline](../../outputs/Zasilacz_8S_D4/README.md). The pinned workflow is recorded in the [project index](../project-index.md). This change applies to REQ-CC, REQ-CONTACT and REQ-ISOLATION. The D4 circuit stays archived. Mechanics and insert CAD are outside this task.

## Electrical behavior

- J3 carries LED+ and LED− only. Remove J2, S1 and S2 from the next schematic. Replace the external TH1 permit contact with automatic local temperature sensing on the power board. The actual sense location and thermal thresholds depend on measured hot spots. The flyback's die thermal protection is separate from the temperature of the transformer, capacitor and PCB.
- Retain the isolated constant-current flyback as a starting point. Target about 270 mA for the intended 8S load. Screen an electronic series switch with current-limited startup and a separate output-voltage limit. Detect open circuit from low LED current, shut off the series path and discharge the contact-side capacitance. Sense actual load current separately from any bleeder or internal supply current. A broken LED string is the same open-load case as unplugging.
- While open or faulted, interrogate the load at low energy. Use a separately current-limited probe path with an independent voltage bound, then prove both voltage and current fall within the healthy low-current 8S envelope before turning on the full output. An open reaches the probe ceiling without sustained load current. A hard short draws probe current at low voltage. Disconnect and discharge between probes. Keep the 470 µF reservoir upstream of the series switch, including during probing. Compare switched discharge with a passive path and account for any probe current consumed by the discharge circuit. Establish probe voltage, current, width, interval and worst-case available energy from the measured load and contact capacitance before capture. A short that occurs while fully on needs its own bounded turn-off path.
- On a short, disconnect full power, pause and retry autonomously. While the short persists, use the limited-energy probe and prevent repeated full-power starts. After a valid load returns, ramp up automatically. Validate retry duty and temperatures over sustained and intermittent shorts. Provide automatic recovery after cooling in the thermal design, subject to validating the real assembly.
- Keep reinforced mains-to-accessible isolation and the existing engineering targets of 30 V DC including peaks in applicable single faults and below 5 V within 100 ms after normal disconnection. A short on an exposed terminal is a fault for energy testing. Touch safety requires separate accessible-contact and touch-current evidence including idle probes, single faults and mains coupling. Include the voltage and energy held in the disconnected insert in the interface review. [IEC LED controlgear scope](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/64713)

## Retained parts and design checks

The TPS26600 is a candidate for independent overvoltage cutoff and controlled connection. Its existing 30.1 kΩ ILIM sets about 0.40 A nominal, above the flyback's 285 mA high static current corner. Changing MODE to auto-retry cannot establish detection of a hard short when the flyback already limits the current. The LYTSwitch-6 has built-in auto-restart, but its detection depends on what it senses at FB and VO. D4 senses RAW at FB and derives VO separately. Credit its behavior only after checking a short beyond the proposed series switch and testing the interaction with the existing thresholds. [TPS2660 datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps2660.pdf) · [LYTSwitch-6 datasheet](https://www.power.com/sites/default/files/documents/lytswitch-6_family_datasheet.pdf)

At the D4 voltage corners, a 27.07 V no-load bus can remain above the candidate eFuse rearm window of 25.53–26.62 V. An overvoltage trip therefore needs a separate recovery analysis. Check nuisance trips during hot connection and ensure a true overvoltage fault cannot be re-enabled into the contacts. Evaluate failed-on series switch and feedback faults independently. Removing S1/S2 makes these electrical safeguards part of the new safety case. [D4 voltage-window analysis](driver-pin-review.md#voltage-window-and-protection-behavior)

The upstream capacitor alone screens to 0.207 J at 564 µF and 27.07 V. This is stored energy, not an estimate of energy delivered during a retry. As a separate discharge screen, 4.7 kΩ at +1% and 10 µF reaches 5 V from 30 V in 85.1 ms after isolation, leaving 14.9 ms for detection and switch-off. Include sense, leakage, component tolerances, and the actual probe and discharge sequence. [Capacitor and discharge analysis](output-protection-study.md)

## Acceptance evidence

- Capture accessible output voltage, touch current and pulse energy at idle, removal, connection, broken string, and output short. Test at cold and hot limits with the intended 8S load across the mains design range. Include precharged RAW, reconnect during shutdown and contact bounce.
- Test output short at first energization, during normal operation, after rapid reconnection and for sustained repetitions at worst ambient. Show that only limited-energy probes continue into a persistent short and that healthy LED current returns automatically when the fault is removed.
- Evaluate the main switch failed on, probe limiter faults, feedback and voltage-limit faults, and thermal sensing faults. Verify the independent insulation barrier, accessible voltage and touch-current limits separately from the functional short tests.

The next schematic task is to size and capture the load-sensing, probe, discharge and cutoff paths and compare their space with the removed interlock and latch circuitry. The 70 × 34 mm envelope remains a study target pending new placement and routing. This architecture update carries no new schematic, prototype measurements or manufacturing authorization.
