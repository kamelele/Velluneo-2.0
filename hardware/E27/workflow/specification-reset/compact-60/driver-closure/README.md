# Compact driver: calculated loss screen and actual remaining design conditions

2026-11-17; input repository 9f6f999aba18ca06b4bdc5ff4d0bf6148bb3fe94. Implementer scope only; no active parts, connectivity, approvals, rules or CAD changed. This candidate is calculated, not a tested converter. It retains the active 31-position circuit and treats T1-C2 as the explicit proposed winding, not a supplier-qualified part.

The old 4 W driver allocation is not an owner requirement or measured loss. The executed component ledger is materially smaller: **1.336–2.156 W** for 47 ohms across the declared 18/24/27 V, 223.4/250/280.9 mA, 207/253 Vac and 50/60 Hz cases. At 24 V/250 mA/253 Vac/50 Hz it gives **1.728 W**. These are conditional screens; they cannot be labelled a guaranteed 2 W limit. In particular, hot MOS resistance, switching energy, magnetic AC loss, capacitor hot ESR/leakage, fuse resistance and bridge forward drop do not have complete guaranteed full-temperature models here. A compact thermal design should evaluate this range and independently bound those terms rather than inherit 4 W or assume this screen closes temperature safety.

## Reproduction and interpretation

Run `python calculate.py` from this directory with numpy 2.2 and numba 0.61.2 available. In the actual session PYTHONPATH was `C:/Users/kamil/Documents/Codex/2026-11-17/zo/work/python-libs`; no external source files are needed to replay the explicitly documented constants. Results contain 108 line/load/resistor cases, 27 independent component sensitivities, three time-step convergence results and three cold-charge pulse cases. Source identities are in sources.json.

The model solves an off-time energy ledger for frequency at the specified output load. It includes finite winding/diode resistance, proposed leakage 20 uH, source-derived D3 conduction equation, auxiliary/gate consumption and selected core/AC loss. It then solves finite on-time resistance loss and a line-frequency capacitor/bridge model. The result is **not** a native UCC28711 current-regulation prediction: output current is an input from the earlier CC screen, and controller demagnetization duty must still agree with source VCCR/VCST after all sampling/offset corrections. This distinction prevents assigning arbitrary frequency and peak-current maxima as simultaneous physical states.

The line engine is a midpoint integrated constant-power load plus the actual 499 kohm equivalent bulk bleeder. It reports line-frequency ripple. Primary switching mean square comes from the finite-Ron pulse integral. The capacitor split subtracts the average-load square and neglects LF/HF covariance; this is an engineering spectral split, **not FFT or switched simulation**. The prior electrical-refinement/capacitor switched RK4 model at 11 W is preserved, but its 11 W input cannot be called the actual compact lamp. Its exact switched/FFT method remains the next numerical cross-check if guaranteed capacitor heating is needed. No invented FFT execution is recorded.

At the representative 24 V case: R1 0.160 W; bulk bleeders 0.237 W; MOS conduction 0.151 W; proposed transformer DC copper P/S 0.024/0.128 W; clamp 0.135 W; output diode 0.186 W; auxiliary/gate 0.123 W. The remainder includes declared ESR/leakage, output bleed, sense, switch and core/AC screens. The source equation for STTH2R02 is 0.68 Iavg + 0.06 Irms². Therefore replacing D3 to recover the previous arbitrary 0.5 W allocation is not justified: much of that apparent saving did not exist.

Rds=16/32/48 ohms, switch energy=0.5/2.5/5 uJ and core/AC=0.05/0.15/0.30 W are separate sensitivities, not guaranteed source bounds. At the representative output these span 1.430–2.120 W. Q1 source 16 ohms maximum applies at its stated 25 C test point; hot multiples are not guaranteed. The 9.5 pF MOS time-equivalent output capacitance must **not** be substituted into 0.5 CV² as energy-equivalent capacitance. Actual turn-off overlap and Eoss need a valid nonlinear source model or later measured qualification.

C1 tan-delta 0.25 at 20 C/120 Hz with Cmin=12 uF gives a 27.63 ohm LF ESR screen only at that source condition. HF 3 ohms is a sensitivity. The Rubycon rated-voltage/20 C leakage formula gives 246.5 uA; extending it to actual hot bus conditions is not guaranteed. C4 ESR 0.12 ohm is specified at 100 kHz, and the screen does not establish its whole spectrum/hot aging. Additional ESR/leak losses are diagnostic ledger additions and are not recursively fed back into input demand; this makes the result unsuitable as a strict total upper bound. The source-derived .2234–.2809 A current endpoints also preserve their original parameter conditions rather than establish complete dynamic regulation.

## R1, fuse and bridge: no premature ECO

The executed no-line-impedance cold-charge screen at 253 Vac and Cmax=18 uF stores 1.152162 J. At 5% minimum R1, 22/33/47 ohms give respectively 17.119/11.413/8.013 A initial current, 0.055127/0.036752/0.025804 A²s and 0.376/0.564/0.804 ms RC time constants. Reducing R1 saves normal heat but increases surge current and I²t; it does not remove charge energy.

The current SCHURTER UMToH native table gives **0.15 A²s typical at 10 In**, not a guaranteed no-open limit for this exponential sub-millisecond pulse. Its table permits a 10 In clearing interval of 10–100 ms; neither that condition nor typical I²t alone establishes coordination at a mains-fed capacitor/MOS/bridge short. Regular Vishay AC05 is not automatically a safety fusible resistor. Its single-pulse graph must be used with E/R, pulse duration, hot derating and resistance, not just its 5 W rating. The smaller AC03 cannot be qualified by average watts: the native single-pulse graph suggests insufficient energy margin around 47 ohms; precise qualification is still required. No downsizing or 22/33 ohm ECO is selected.

A concrete alternative worth supplier qualification is Vishay **AC05-CS**, a different safety-fusible family with UL1412 recognition E362452. The official product page identifies its separate datasheet `https://www.vishay.com/docs/28909/acxxcs.pdf` and application note. That identity is a sourcing direction, **not** an interchangeable part or qualified ECO: exact resistance/order code, surge endurance, maximum fault-opening energy/time, dimensional lead form and source test conditions must be reconciled before changing R1. No purchase/contact was made. The exact Diodes MB10S pulse curve and source line impedance/fault supply category also remain necessary for a numeric bridge/fuse coordination conclusion; generic MB10S ratings from another maker are not substituted.

## Controller, reference and stage separation

The official TI SLUUA12 reference is actual measured evidence for its **5 V/1 A** design, not this 24 V converter. It supplies 115/230 V startup waveforms, load transients and full-load ripple, with about 76% typical nominal full-load efficiency. This supports the plausibility of losses around 1.6 W at 5 W, without transferring its pass result to Velluneo. Its transformer, MOSFET, current shunt, gate resistor, output diode, CDD and input fusible resistor differ. Its startup images and graphs do not supply a hard-output-short fault test. Do not cite them as one.

The absence of the user's TINA-TI installation is **not a product requirement**. A sufficiently bounded independent device/state/energy model or a demonstrably applicable qualified reference can satisfy a design calculation; matched prototype tests then belong to physical qualification before release. However, the available encrypted UCC28710 model does not implement the exact UCC28711 external NTC behavior, and the measured EVM's substantially different power train does not bound the outstanding Velluneo fault train. Therefore existing CTRL-F01–F07 source-coverage findings remain unresolved for their electrical reasons, rather than because a named program is missing.

Before layout freeze, the design must specify source-valid maximum fault peak/flux, clamp/output/VDD limits, finite retry energy and recovery state for actual short/open/feedback faults. Actual temperature/EMI/hipot/short tests are correctly later physical gates. A missing design bound is not automatically deferable as a test. The existing numerical discharge (35 to 7.7 V through R13/CDD in at most 1.530405 s) assumes no continuing injection. With zero guaranteed internal draw, continuing VDD injection must remain below 7.5416 uA to reach that threshold at all. It is not a minimum retry period or a guaranteed short-circuit heat limit.

Normal passive heat may prove RT1-open benign after the compact assembly thermal network and genuine worst losses are qualified. It does not yet bound panel short plus lost NTC or other specified fault combinations. Internal shutdown around typical 165 C is not whole-lamp protection. The prior seven-part TMP708/TPS7A16 proposal remains unapplied because its startup/charge/interface/lag conditions are incomplete; adding it by default would not close those conditions. Do not remove RT1-open or recovery requirements to make the candidate pass.
