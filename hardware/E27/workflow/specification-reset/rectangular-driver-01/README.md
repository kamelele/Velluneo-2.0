# RECT-01 — rectangular driver, round LED insert

This corrects the coordinator's mistaken interpretation. The owner requires a rectangular power-driver PCB; only the LED insert is round. The whole lamp still targets diameter 60 mm and full height 120 mm. R-BOARD-FORM records this actual correction. The earlier 51 mm circular driver study and its circle-specific courtyard proposals are superseded, not selected or applied. Prior electrical and heat calculations retain their limited scope.

The corrected **32 × 60 mm rectangular driver** is a dimensional trial. Its long direction runs along the proposed lamp axis: secondary output towards the LED insert, AC input towards the E27 cap. Board width plus populated thickness must fit the housing; the board's plan diagonal is not the lamp diameter constraint in this orientation. Longitudinal mounting is an engineering proposal. The owner specified the forms, not these exact board dimensions or mounting. The existing round 48 mm LED-insert proposal remains separate.

All **31 positions and 79 pads** are assigned using the unchanged native footprints. Saved checks find no courtyard/allocation overrun, same-face box collision or opposite-face through-hole pad/joint obstacle collision in this trial. Minimum projected primary/secondary metal-box distance is 9.23 mm; minimum pad-box distance to the board edge is 0.60 mm. These use explicit working allowances, not adopted normative creepage or qualified solder rules. Native KiCad transformations independently match all 79 saved pad boxes.

The native rectangular R1/C1 courtyards remain intact. No footprint-contour change is needed merely to fit this rectangular study. The transformer still uses an unverified finished 30 × 30 × 18 mm allocation. C1's front-side pin/joint projections sit between T1 and R1; its back-side body has some opposite-face projection overlap with R1, so thermal coupling remains unqualified. C4 is on the front beyond T1's secondary end. Both drawings use common front/global XY; the back is projected through the board.

This does not establish complete 3D fit, tolerances, functional loops, routing, thermal performance or readiness for PCB entry. In particular, the earlier horizontal-disc axial stack and stock 30 mm cooler scenario cannot be reused as verified for a longitudinal board. Actual cap, insulating cup, supports, lead forming, strain relief, cooler, diffuser, service interfaces and all component heights need a coherent assembly design. The old 18 mm T1 and 20 mm C1 height allocations are not guaranteed source maxima.

No native product PCB, routing, DRC or stage approval is created. Electrical CAD, the approved physical graph, values, libraries and rules are unchanged. Actual U05–U09 and remaining magnetic, fault, thermal, insulation and process blockers stay open. Four independent read-only roles review the same frozen correction packet; their recommendations do not grant owner stage approval.
